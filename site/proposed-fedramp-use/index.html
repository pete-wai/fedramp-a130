Proposal & working paper · 26 October 2026

Using cloud services through FedRAMP

A signed CSA for the cloud service. A separate ATO for the agency system.

This report reviews a proposed agency workflow and turns it into an eight-step workbook. It is an independent analysis, not official FedRAMP guidance or an authorization.

Review the six proposed stepsStart the workbook ↓

The core premise

The proposal is a new FedRAMP-specific Cloud Service Authorization (CSA) process that agencies would follow for cloud services used by their federal information systems. Each CSA would be a distinct signed agency decision, referenced from SA-9, SA-4, and other applicable controls. The agency would separately authorize its federal information system through the FISMA ATO process.

The FedRAMP Act’s process and reuse authorities, M-24-15, and NIST’s external-service model provide a basis for evaluating this design. The CSA would operate within the agency system’s lifecycle, with its own decision scope and signed record. Agency monitoring would connect service evidence and CSA conditions to the system’s continued authorization.

What would be new: a FedRAMP-defined agency CSA procedure and signed decision, separate from the agency system ATO and from the provider’s FedRAMP authorization or certification. SA-9, SA-4, and other control records would reference the CSA. The CSA would have its own signing authority, decision criteria, and lifecycle under that new process.

Published FedRAMP guidance supports reuse and agency system authorization. The cited guidance does not establish the distinct CSA proposed here. The report separates that proposed process from existing requirements and identifies what FedRAMP would need to define.

Point of concern · Commercial does not automatically mean outside federal system scope

The workflow does not depend on a blanket claim that commercial cloud services cannot operate on the government’s behalf. “External” in SA-9 concerns control implementation and assessment. FISMA, A-130, and FedRAMP have their own wording and scope. Record the facts and applicable responsibilities instead of using the service’s commercial status as an exemption.

Review of the six proposed steps

Step 4 below reflects the clarified proposal: agencies perform a separate, signed CSA under a new FedRAMP process. Step 5 remains the agency system’s FISMA ATO. The expanded workbook adds preparation, agreements, control references, and connected monitoring.

  1. Begin with information and use

    Aligned. Add ownership, information flows, non-security obligations, and a FedRAMP scope determination. See workbook step

  2. Categorize the agency system and tailor controls

    Aligned. Derive the category from information and mission harm; define a real boundary and allocate responsibilities. See workbook step

  3. Evaluate external services using FedRAMP evidence

    Aligned with conditions. Presume provider assessment adequate; document fit and customer work without repeating the assessment. See workbook step

  4. Perform a Cloud Service Authorization through a new FedRAMP process

    Proposed new procedure. Agencies would issue a distinct signed CSA for cloud services used by their agency information system, document the applicable requirements, and reference the CSA from SA-9, SA-4, and other controls. The system ATO remains separate. See workbook step

  5. Implement, assess, and authorize the agency system

    Aligned. Complete the system’s FISMA assessment and ATO, using signed CSAs as supporting decisions and assessing agency implementation and integrations. See workbook step

  6. Monitor the service and agency system

    Aligned if connected. Feed FedRAMP evidence into agency monitoring, ongoing risk acceptance, and change/exit decisions. See workbook step

What the law lets FedRAMP standardize

The 2022 Act gives GSA a program and process mandate. M-24-15 replaces the 2011 FedRAMP memorandum and assigns FedRAMP the process and criteria for a FedRAMP authorization. Those authorities support a reusable service-assurance process, templates, boundary guidance, and monitoring procedures. They are the basis for considering the proposed agency CSA. The general process mandate does not, by itself, specify this new signed agency decision; its adoption would need to follow the respective GSA, Board, and OMB roles and remain consistent with the governing requirements.

What those changes preserve

The Act expressly preserves agency FISMA responsibility in §3613(e)(2), quoted in step 4. M-24-15 footnote 7 preserves other information-management, records, privacy, and cybersecurity obligations, quoted in step 7. Neither source supplies a blanket repeal of A-130 or NIST. The Act also requires consistency with NIST standards and guidelines and relevant statutes.

Point of concern · Apply newer reuse rules to older NIST procedures

SP 800-37 Rev. 2 Appendix F allows requests for additional controls or information when evaluating a shared service. For FedRAMP use, that older general procedure must be read with the later statutory presumption of adequacy, demonstrable-need provision, M-24-15, and current FedRAMP notification rules. Older discretion is not permission to duplicate the provider assessment.

Three decisions to keep distinct

FedRAMP authorization / certification
The program’s determination about an offering. The Act and M-24-15 use “authorization”; the current 2026 program pages use “certification.” Preserve those terms in citations and identify the specific package/status being reused.
Agency Cloud Service Authorization — proposed
A distinct signed agency decision under a new FedRAMP process for a cloud service used by an identified agency information system. It documents requirements, evidence, responsibilities, conditions, and accepted service-use risk. SA-9, SA-4, and other applicable system control records reference its identifier and version.
Agency system ATO
The separate authorization of the federal information system under the FISMA framework. It relies on the signed CSAs and other evidence while assessing the system as a whole. Existing NIST ATU guidance is quoted later for comparison; an ATU is not the CSA proposed here.

Agency-use workbook

Use one worksheet per agency use and service; link related records when a system uses several services. Work through the eight records below. Steps 2–5 are iterative: evidence or design changes can send you back. The proposed CSA and the agency system ATO have separate scopes. For a new system, operational use waits for its ATO. Within an existing ATO lifecycle, a new CSA also requires review of whether the system authorization still covers the changed use.

01

RMF · Prepare

Define the work, information, and scope

Responsible participants: Mission owner · information owner · security, privacy, records, and acquisition staff

Start with the agency’s work, not a provider’s control package. Describe what people will do, which information will move, and what harm could follow from disclosure, alteration, loss, or misuse. Identify the officials who own the information and accept risk.

Your step 1, with a scope and ownership check added.

What the agency does

  • Map creation, collection, processing, storage, sharing, and disposal. Include account details, logs, metadata, exports, integrations, and information sent to other providers. State permitted and prohibited uses.
  • Identify applicable legal, privacy, records, accessibility, acquisition, and agency requirements. Security categorization alone does not answer all of these questions.
  • Decide whether this use falls within M-24-15 §III and current FedRAMP scope guidance. Record the applicable inclusion or exclusion, the specific offering and deployment, and who approved the determination. Resolve uncertainty with agency officials and FedRAMP.

Why this fits

FISMA protects both agency information and the systems described in the statute. M-24-15 makes scope depend on handling federal information on an agency’s behalf, with listed exclusions. A service’s commercial status does not answer that scope question.

Point of concern · Do not turn “external” into an exemption

A boundary decision is not a ruling that FISMA, A-130, or FedRAMP cannot apply. The proposal works without asserting that every commercial cloud is outside the statutory system definition. Out-of-scope services still need the protections applicable to the agency’s information and use.

Supporting language

What task, users, and uses are allowed? What is prohibited?

02

RMF · Categorize

Categorize information and the agency system

Responsible participants: Information owner · system owner · authorizing official or delegate

Assess confidentiality, integrity, and availability separately for each information type. Then categorize the agency system that will support the work. Record the harm behind each rating.

Your steps 1–2, keeping the information and system decisions distinct.

What the agency does

  • Use FIPS 199 and relevant SP 800-60 information-type guidance. Consider mission impact, people affected, aggregated information, and system processing needs. Public information can still require strong integrity and availability protection.
  • Derive the system’s security category from its information types, taking the highest impact for each objective. FIPS 200 then uses the system impact level in baseline selection.
  • Document and obtain the required agency review of the categorization. Revisit it if the design, information, or use changes. Assess privacy and other risks alongside the security category.

Why this fits

FISMA §3554(a)(2) connects harm assessment to protection levels and implementation. FIPS 199 supplies separate information-type and system categories; FIPS 200 connects the system category to control selection.

Point of concern · A certification class is not a FIPS category

Do not choose the agency’s impact level to match an available service or its FedRAMP certification class. Current FedRAMP guidance distinguishes assurance classes from the agency’s security categorization.

Supporting language

Record 2 · Categorize information and the agency system

For each type: confidentiality / integrity / availability impact; concrete harm; rationale; approving official.

Not recorded.

System name, impact by objective, overall impact, aggregation considerations, approval/date, and separate privacy risks.

Not recorded.

03

RMF · Prepare + Select

Set the boundary and allocate controls

Responsible participants: System owner · security/privacy architects · common control providers

Describe a real agency system: its people, procedures, technology, and dependencies. Select and tailor controls for that system. Allocate each protection to the agency, a common control provider, an external service, or a combination.

Your step 2, with architecture and shared responsibilities made explicit.

What the agency does

  • Draw the agency authorization boundary, provider certification boundary, and information exchanges. Explain which components are included, which services are external, and how the agency depends on them. Boundaries can differ without creating a gap in responsibility.
  • Select the applicable baseline from SP 800-53B, use the current SP 800-53 catalog, and document tailoring, parameters, supplemental controls, and privacy requirements. FIPS 200’s older citation to SP 800-53 is read with its instruction to use the current publication as amended.
  • Define required capabilities before comparing providers: identity, access, logging, recovery, data protection, incident coordination, and other needs. Record the customer work necessary to use provider capabilities. Revisit the design when a service cannot support a necessary capability.

Why this fits

RMF §2.5 defines the boundary around management and responsibilities. Task S-3 allocates controls to system elements and the environment. SA-9 supplies external-service oversight and monitoring requirements within this wider control set.

Point of concern · Neither the provider nor an empty container is the agency system

Do not copy a provider’s entire system into an agency SSP. Do not invent a nominal enclosing system just to avoid describing the actual use. For a simple SaaS use, the agency’s accounts, configurations, information handling, personnel, and procedures still need a meaningful boundary and authorization path.

Supporting language

Record 3 · Set the boundary and allocate controls

Describe or link the diagram: agency resources, provider offering, interfaces, inherited services, and exclusions with reasons.

Not recorded.

For each requirement/control: rationale, parameter, agency/provider/shared owner, implementation, evidence needed, and monitoring owner. Record SA-9 oversight and monitoring assignments.

Not recorded.

04

RMF · Select + risk assessment

Reuse FedRAMP evidence and decide whether the service fits

Responsible participants: System owner · assessors · security/privacy staff · acquisition team

Compare candidate services against the agency’s needs. Reuse the FedRAMP package for the provider’s assessed work. Spend agency effort on whether the specific offering, configuration, dependencies, and proposed use meet the mission and protection needs.

Your step 3, with the statutory presumption of adequacy applied.

What the agency does

  • Confirm the exact certified or authorized offering, boundary, included features, status, applicable assurance information, and ongoing evidence. Check whether the planned deployment is covered. Use current FedRAMP guidance for the assurance evidence; do not invent a certification-class-to-FIPS-impact equivalence. A related product name or marketplace entry is not enough.
  • Review secure configuration guidance, responsibility allocations, assessment findings, ongoing reports, inherited services, and constraints. Distinguish provider evidence, agency implementation evidence, and unanswered questions.
  • Use the presumption of adequacy. Ask clarifying questions. If added provider requirements or information are needed, record the demonstrable need and use the required agency determination and FedRAMP notification procedures. Collaborate with FedRAMP on package conflicts; document a wholly or substantially deficient package determination where applicable.
  • Select a service that supports the required capabilities, or revise the architecture or use. Track unresolved issues and acquisition conditions. Selection is not permission to begin processing information.

Why this fits

44 USC 3613(a)(3) requires reuse to the extent practicable. Section 3613(e) presumes the assessment adequate while preserving FISMA duties and demonstrable additional needs. M-24-15 §IV and current Agency Use rules implement that balance.

Point of concern · Do not recreate the provider assessment under SA-9

Selecting controls for the agency system does not justify sending the provider a new agency checklist. Separate a customer-side configuration or integration need from an additional provider security requirement. Use the current FedRAMP process for the latter; a routine preference is not automatically a demonstrable need.

Supporting language

Record 4 · Reuse FedRAMP evidence and decide whether the service fits

Provider/service/version/deployment, package identifier and date, status/class or impact basis as applicable, features and inherited services covered, reviewers, and evidence links.

Not recorded.

Required capability → provider evidence → agency configuration/work → residual gap/risk. Identify stale or missing evidence.

Not recorded.

Distinguish clarification from added requirements. State demonstrable need, determining official, notification/reference, package conflicts, and resolution. Enter “none” when appropriate.

Not recorded.

05

RMF · Proposed FedRAMP CSA process · supports Select and Implement

Perform and sign a Cloud Service Authorization

Responsible participants: Agency CSA signing official · system owner · acquisition, security, and privacy staff

Under this proposal, the agency follows a new FedRAMP-specific process to authorize a cloud service for use by its agency information system. The result is a signed Cloud Service Authorization (CSA). The agency references that CSA from its system documentation for SA-9, SA-4, and other applicable controls.

Your revised step 4: a distinct, signed CSA under a new FedRAMP process, separate from the agency system ATO.

What the agency does

  • Use the FedRAMP-defined CSA procedure to review the service, reuse provider assessment evidence, resolve agency-specific requirements, and make a service-level risk decision for the identified agency system. This is an agency CSA, distinct from the provider’s FedRAMP authorization or certification.
  • Document the service and approved information/use, required protections, provider and agency responsibilities, configuration and integration conditions, evidence relied on, residual risks, monitoring duties, and review or termination triggers. Identify the signing official and authority required by the new process.
  • Set agreements for security/privacy, incidents, recovery, evidence access, information exchanges, records, retention/deletion, and exit. Reference the signed CSA by identifier and version from SA-9 external-service documentation, SA-4 acquisition documentation, and other applicable controls such as CA-3. Each control entry should still explain its own implementation and responsibilities.
  • Issue a distinct signed CSA decision and retain its supporting record. Link it to the agency system and its ATO package. For a new system, the CSA can precede the completed system ATO, with operational use contingent on that ATO. For an already authorized system, perform the CSA within its lifecycle and determine whether the proposed use requires an ATO update or other agency risk decision.

Why this fits

The Act’s process, template, and reuse authorities and M-24-15 provide a basis for proposing this procedure. A-130 and SA-4, SA-9, and CA-3 support the agreements, responsibilities, and evidence it would document. The cited materials do not themselves establish this distinct signed agency CSA; FedRAMP would need to adopt and define it through the applicable governance process.

Point of concern · FedRAMP must define the new decision, not just its name

The new process needs a defined scope, signing authority, decision standard, required evidence, signature and record requirements, monitoring and revocation rules, and relationship to the system ATO. It must preserve the statutory presumption of adequacy and agency FISMA duties. SA-9 and SA-4 document the CSA’s implementation; they do not independently create the CSA process.

Supporting language

Record 5 · Perform and sign a Cloud Service Authorization

Contract/agreement reference → requirement → responsible party → evidence/remedy. Map the CSA identifier and version to SA-9, SA-4, CA-3, and other applicable agency-system control entries.

Not recorded.

Status (draft, approved, denied, or deferred); CSA ID/version; FedRAMP CSA procedure/version; agency system ID; service and approved information/use; requirements, responsibilities, evidence, residual risk, conditions and monitoring; signing official/authority, signature/date and signed-record location; linked system ATO. This worksheet does not sign the CSA.

Not recorded.

06

RMF · Implement + Assess

Configure, integrate, and assess the agency’s implementation

Responsible participants: System owner · implementers · control assessors

Build or configure the agency’s use, implement customer responsibilities, and assess that implementation. Reuse provider evidence where it applies. Check that the combined arrangement works, not merely that each party has documents.

Your step 5, with implementation evidence separated from provider evidence.

What the agency does

  • Implement the approved design, agency controls, shared controls, configuration settings, access rules, training, and operating procedures. Record actual implementation in the agency security/privacy plans and link SA-9, SA-4, and other applicable entries to the signed CSA. Reconcile implementation changes with CSA conditions before operational use.
  • Test relevant integrations and agency responsibilities: account lifecycle, privileged access, information sharing, log delivery, incident coordination, recovery, and exit capabilities. Confirm that claimed inherited capabilities are available in the selected service and deployment.
  • Prepare assessment results, remediation plans, and residual-risk analysis. Link provider findings that affect agency risk to the agency decision; do not duplicate every provider POA&M as if it were agency work.
  • Revisit the design or service selection if the combined controls are inadequate. Use only an appropriately approved test or pilot arrangement before operational authorization.

Why this fits

FISMA requires implementation and periodic evaluation. A-130 requires contractor oversight. NIST’s reuse guidance still requires consideration of integration risk, and current FedRAMP guidance tells agencies to test their implementation.

Point of concern · A signed CSA does not authorize an unfinished agency system

The CSA is a distinct service decision under the proposed FedRAMP process. The agency still has to implement and assess the system that uses the service. Operational use must be covered by the agency system’s ATO; a CSA alone does not supply that authorization. A limited pilot has its own conditions.

Supporting language

Record 6 · Configure, integrate, and assess the agency’s implementation

Implemented settings/procedures, evidence links, assessor/date, provider evidence reused, inherited capabilities checked, and integration tests/results.

Not recorded.

Finding → affected mission/information → owner → mitigation or acceptance → due date. Link provider remediation and record any agency compensating actions.

Not recorded.

07

RMF · Authorize

Authorize the agency information system under FISMA

Responsible participants: Agency authorizing official

The agency authorizing official makes the ATO decision for the federal information system as a whole. The system authorization package incorporates the signed CSA for each cloud service, along with evidence for agency controls, integrations, and the rest of the system.

Your step 5: the agency system ATO remains a separate decision under FISMA, supported by the signed CSA records.

What the agency does

  • Complete the agency system assessment and authorization package under the applicable FISMA, OMB, and NIST processes. Identify the system boundary, mission and information, all relevant controls, integration evidence, residual risks, and the signed CSA records being relied on.
  • Issue or update the agency system ATO through agency procedures. Reference the CSA identifiers and versions and ensure their conditions are reflected in the system design, SA-9, SA-4, other applicable controls, and monitoring plan. The CSA and ATO remain distinct signed decisions with different scopes.
  • Keep existing terminology clear. M-24-15 and SP 800-37 also describe an ATU reuse route, quoted below for comparison. This proposal uses a system ATO plus a new CSA; it does not rename an ATU as a CSA or claim that the existing ATU provisions already establish the new process.
  • Submit the agency ATO letter and supplementary information to FedRAMP as currently required. Any CSA-specific submission or registration requirement would need to be established in the new process. If system risk is unacceptable, resolve the issue, restrict the use, or deny the ATO even if a service has a CSA.

Why this fits

A-130 assigns formal system authorization to senior federal officials. The FedRAMP Act preserves agency FISMA responsibility and requires submission of the agency ATO based on FedRAMP authorization. The proposed CSA supplies a separate service decision and evidence within that system lifecycle; it does not discharge the system-level duties.

Point of concern · Separate decisions should reuse the same evidence

The CSA addresses the cloud service’s approved use, requirements, and responsibilities. The ATO addresses the agency system as a whole, including integration and combined risk. FedRAMP would need to make the distinction operational so agencies can reference CSA work in the ATO package without repeating the provider assessment or issuing an ATO for the provider’s offering.

Supporting language

Record 7 · Authorize the agency information system under FISMA

System/package ID; ATO official, decision/date and signed-record location; approved scope, information, configuration, residual risk and conditions; linked CSA IDs/versions and SA-9/SA-4/other control references; review triggers. Record denial, deferral, or the treatment of a change to an existing ATO.

Not recorded.

Date/reference and agency ATO letter/supplementary information supplied to FedRAMP, or why not applicable. Separately identify any CSA reporting that the new procedure would require; do not assume it replaces the ATO submission.

Not recorded.

08

RMF · Monitor + disposal

Monitor both, respond to change, and plan exit

Responsible participants: System owner · monitoring staff · authorizing official · service contacts

Use FedRAMP’s ongoing service evidence in the agency’s continuous monitoring program. Monitor the agency’s configuration, accounts, integrations, and procedures too. Use both streams to maintain the CSA’s conditions and inform the agency system’s ongoing ATO decision.

Your step 6, with feedback, change response, and exit added.

What the agency does

  • Specify evidence sources, review cadence, owners, escalation paths, and durable contacts. Include relevant inherited service evidence. Review provider reports and findings for their effect on the agency’s authorized use.
  • Monitor customer controls, configuration drift, logs, account changes, privacy/records handling, incidents, and agency remediation. Record what the agency accepted, changed, or escalated in response to service evidence.
  • Reassess when information, mission, deployment, features, integrations, provider posture, certification status, or threats change. Notify FedRAMP and the provider when required; decide whether to continue, restrict, suspend, or end use. Under the proposed process, record any CSA revision or revocation and its effect on the system ATO.
  • Plan export, retention, deletion, account closure, key handling, transition, and evidence of completion before exit. Disposal is part of the information lifecycle, not a task left to the provider’s default retention policy.

Why this fits

SA-9 requires ongoing external-provider monitoring and leaves risk with the authorizing official. M-24-15 supplies a shared monitoring framework; current FedRAMP guidance expressly says it does not replace agency monitoring.

Point of concern · Two evidence streams must not become two disconnected programs

FedRAMP monitoring is not a transfer of the agency’s risk responsibility. Equally, agency monitoring should not recreate the provider’s program or demand advance approval for every service change. Monitor the provider’s change process and the effects on the agency’s use, following current FedRAMP procedures.

Supporting language

Record 8 · Monitor both, respond to change, and plan exit

Evidence/report → review frequency → reviewer → trigger/threshold → action/official. Include provider and agency sources, inherited services, and continuity of contacts.

Not recorded.

Triggers for re-review, restrictions or suspension; incident coordination/notification; export/retention/deletion; account/key closure; transition owner; completion evidence.

Not recorded.

What the completed record should show

The authorizing official should be able to trace the decision from the agency’s information and mission to the selected service, evidence, customer responsibilities, assessment results, conditions, and ongoing monitoring. The completed package should identify two separate signed decisions: the service CSA under the proposed FedRAMP procedure and the agency system ATO. This worksheet helps assemble and cross-reference them; exporting it does not sign either decision.

  • Scope: the actual agency use, information, system boundary, and provider offering.
  • Protection: categorization, selected controls, allocation, agreements, and verified implementation.
  • Evidence: FedRAMP material reused, agency assessment, unresolved issues, and justified additions.
  • Decisions: the CSA signing official, authority, signature/date and record; the separate system ATO and its official; CSA references in SA-9, SA-4, and other controls; and the applicable submission requirements.
  • Continued use: monitoring owners, evidence cadence, escalation, change triggers, and exit arrangements.
