44 USC §3575 — Findings

Congress explains why sharing business data among designated statistical agencies can improve statistics and reduce reporting burdens.

Congress

Evidence Act 2018

Section source laws · Explore its earlier history.

Statutory text

The Congress finds the following:

(1)

Federal statistics are an important source of information for public and private decision-makers such as policymakers, consumers, businesses, investors, and workers.

Context and history: 44 USC 3575(1)

Section history and amendment instructions · Compare earlier editions · Link to this provision

(2)

Federal statistical agencies should continuously seek to improve their efficiency. Statutory constraints limit the ability of these agencies to share data and thus to achieve higher efficiency for Federal statistical programs.

Context and history: 44 USC 3575(2)

Section history and amendment instructions · Compare earlier editions · Link to this provision

(3)

The quality of Federal statistics depends on the willingness of businesses to respond to statistical surveys. Reducing reporting burdens will increase response rates, and therefore lead to more accurate characterizations of the economy.

Context and history: 44 USC 3575(3)

Section history and amendment instructions · Compare earlier editions · Link to this provision

(4)

Enhanced sharing of business data among the Bureau of the Census, the Bureau of Economic Analysis, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics for exclusively statistical purposes will improve their ability to track more accurately the large and rapidly changing nature of United States business. In particular, the statistical agencies will be able to better ensure that businesses are consistently classified in appropriate industries, resolve data anomalies, produce statistical samples that are consistently adjusted for the entry and exit of new businesses in a timely manner, and correct faulty reporting errors quickly and efficiently.

Context and history: 44 USC 3575(4)

Section history and amendment instructions · Compare earlier editions · Link to this provision

(5)

Congress enacted the International Investment and Trade in Services Survey Act (Public Law 94–472), which allowed the Bureau of the Census, the Bureau of Economic Analysis, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics to share data on foreign-owned companies. The Act not only expanded detailed industry coverage from 135 industries to over 800 industries with no increase in the data collected from respondents but also demonstrated how data sharing can result in the creation of valuable data products.

Context and history: 44 USC 3575(5)

Section history and amendment instructions · Compare earlier editions · Link to this provision

(6)

With part B of this subchapter, the sharing of business data among the Bureau of the Census, the Bureau of Economic Analysis, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics continues to ensure the highest level of confidentiality for respondents to statistical surveys.

Context and history: 44 USC 3575(6)
