44 USC §3555 — Annual independent evaluation

Each agency receives an annual independent evaluation of its security program and practices. Inspectors General usually perform or arrange that evaluation; the statute separately addresses agencies without IGs, national security systems, reporting, and GAO’s role.

Statutory text

(a)

In General.—

(1)

Each year each agency shall have performed an independent evaluation of the information security program and practices of that agency to determine the effectiveness of such program and practices.

Context and history: 44 USC 3555(a)(1)

Annual independent security evaluations

Statutory origin: GISRA 2000 · Enacted 2000-10-30

GISRA required an independent evaluation each year. The 2002 laws revised the evaluation requirements; FISMA 2014 continued them under today’s section number.

(b)

Independent Auditor.—

Subject to subsection (c)—

(1)

for each agency with an Inspector General appointed under chapter 4 of title 5, the annual evaluation required by this section shall be performed by the Inspector General or by an independent external auditor, as determined by the Inspector General of the agency; and

Context and history: 44 USC 3555(b)(1)

A citation changed in 2022; the duty did not begin then

The 2022 amendment replaced the reference to the Inspector General Act with Title 5, chapter 4. Annual independent security evaluations appeared in GISRA in 2000 and continued through the 2002 and 2014 legislation.

The IG’s role in the annual evaluation

Statutory origin: GISRA 2000 · Enacted 2000-10-30

GISRA assigned the IG a role in performing or arranging the evaluation, with special rules for sensitive systems. The current auditor language developed through the 2002 and 2014 provisions.

(d)

Existing Evaluations.—

The evaluation required by this section may be based in whole or in part on an audit, evaluation, or report relating to programs or practices of the applicable agency.

Context and history: 44 USC 3555(d)

Existing work can support the evaluation

The evaluator may use an audit, evaluation, or report about the agency’s programs or practices. This permits reuse without removing the annual evaluation requirement.

(f)

Protection of Information.—

Agencies and evaluators shall take appropriate steps to ensure the protection of information which, if disclosed, may adversely affect information security. Such protections shall be commensurate with the risk and comply with all applicable laws and regulations.

Context and history: 44 USC 3555(f)

Protect sensitive evaluation information

Agencies and evaluators protect information whose disclosure could harm information security. The protections must reflect risk and comply with applicable laws and regulations.

(i)

Assessment Technical Assistance.—

The Comptroller General may provide technical assistance to an Inspector General or the head of an agency, as applicable, to assist the Inspector General or head of an agency in carrying out the duties under this section, including by testing information security controls and procedures.

Context and history: 44 USC 3555(i)

GAO may assist evaluators

GAO may provide technical assistance to IGs or agency heads, including testing controls and procedures.

Section history and amendment instructions · Compare earlier editions · Link to this provision

(j)

Guidance.—

The Director, in consultation with the Secretary, the Chief Information Officers Council established under section 3603, the Council of the Inspectors General on Integrity and Efficiency, and other interested parties as appropriate, shall ensure the development of guidance for evaluating the effectiveness of an information security program and practices.

Context and history: 44 USC 3555(j)

OMB coordinates evaluation guidance

OMB develops evaluation guidance in consultation with DHS, the CIO Council, CIGIE, and other appropriate participants. Reporting metrics implement this assignment; they do not create the underlying annual duty.
