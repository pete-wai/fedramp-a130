Who evaluates agency security?

Agency management, Inspectors General, OMB, and GAO have different jobs. The annual independent evaluation connects those responsibilities without transferring ownership of the security program away from the agency.

An independent evaluation every year

Each agency must have its information-security program and practices independently evaluated each year. The evaluation tests a representative subset of systems and assesses the effectiveness of the agency’s policies and practices. It is broader than an assessment of one system or one cloud service. 44 USC §3555(a).

For agencies with an Inspector General, the IG performs the evaluation or determines whether an independent external auditor will do it. For other agencies, the agency head engages an independent external auditor. National security systems have a separate arrangement under which the agency head designates the evaluator. §3555(b)–(c).

Management’s testing continues alongside the IG evaluation

Agency leadership operates the security program. FISMA requires periodic testing and evaluation of controls, with a frequency based on risk and no less than annually. Management also tracks remediation, handles incidents, and maintains continuity plans. The independent evaluation assesses the program’s effectiveness; it does not take over management’s responsibilities. §3554(b).

Evaluators may use existing audits, evaluations, and reports. That allows relevant work to support the annual evaluation without changing who is responsible for it. §3555(d).

Results reach OMB and Congress

The agency head submits evaluation results to OMB by the date OMB establishes. OMB summarizes those results in its report to Congress. The statute protects sensitive information and specifies special treatment for national-security and intelligence-related information. §3555(e)–(g).

IGs also have reporting responsibilities under their general statute, now codified in Title 5. Those duties coexist with the specific FISMA evaluation process. 5 USC §404 and §405.

Guidance coordinates how evaluations are performed

OMB must ensure development of evaluation guidance in consultation with DHS, the CIO Council, the Council of the Inspectors General on Integrity and Efficiency, and other appropriate participants. Annual reporting instructions and metrics give evaluators a common framework. The statutory duty remains the evaluation required by §3555. §3555(j).

GAO adds congressional oversight

The Comptroller General periodically evaluates agency security policies and implementation of FISMA, reports to Congress, and may provide technical assistance to evaluators. GAO’s government-wide work is distinct from each agency’s annual independent evaluation. §3555(h)–(i).

The FedRAMP statute separately requires program reporting and a GAO report. Independent assessment of a cloud service under FedRAMP serves a different purpose from evaluating an agency’s full security program. §3611 and §3615.

The annual duty predates the current section number

GISRA required annual independent evaluations in 2000. FISMA 2002 continued the requirement, and FISMA 2014 placed it in today’s §3555. A 2022 amendment changed the citation to the Inspector General statute after its recodification in Title 5. That citation change did not create a new annual evaluation duty.
