Where federal information-security duties come from

Code through September 30, 2026 · Edition and coverage

Federal information-security duties come from several laws. The Paperwork Reduction Act assigns information-management responsibilities; the Federal Information Security Modernization Act (FISMA) specifies security programs and oversight; other statutes establish standards, operational powers, privacy safeguards, and cloud authorization.

Names used throughout the guide
U.S. Code
Federal statutes organized by subject. Source credits and amendment notes show the enactments behind each section.
OMB
Office of Management and Budget; oversees government-wide information policy, budgets, and agency security implementation.
PRA
Paperwork Reduction Act; governs federal information management as well as collections from the public.
GISRA
Government Information Security Reform provisions enacted in 2000.
FISMA

National Institute of Standards and Technology and the Department of Commerce; develop and issue federal information-system standards under their statutory authorities.
FIPS
Federal Information Processing Standards; the applicable standard specifies its scope and requirements.
Special Publication
A NIST publication series. Its requirements must be understood with the law, policy, standard, or contract that calls for its use.
DHS and CISA
Department of Homeland Security and its Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency; administer specified security implementation and operational functions.
CIO
Chief Information Officer; advises agency leadership and carries out assigned information-management and security responsibilities.
IG and CIGIE
Inspector General, and the Council of the Inspectors General on Integrity and Efficiency; perform and coordinate independent oversight.
GAO

General Services Administration, and the Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program for cloud products and services.

Binding operational directive; a compulsory direction within the scope and conditions of 44 USC §§3552–3553.
