How today’s duties took shape

A duty’s first appearance, its latest amendment, and its current section number can have different dates. Follow the main changes below, then compare the wording of individual sections and clauses.

Information management came before FISMA

The Paperwork Reduction Act of 1980 gave OMB responsibilities for federal information policy, including safeguards for information. The 1995 PRA restated chapter 35. Today, §3504(g) assigns OMB privacy and security oversight, while §3506(g) requires agencies to implement the applicable policies. A-130 carries these information-management responsibilities into agency practice.

PRA 1980, §2, former §3504(f) · PRA 1995, §2

GISRA established annual program evaluations

In 2000, Congress enacted the Government Information Security Reform provisions, commonly called GISRA. The law required agency security programs and annual independent evaluations in former §§3534 and 3535. In 2002, the Homeland Security Act and the E-Government Act enacted overlapping security provisions. The E-Government Act’s FISMA title used §§3541–3549; former §3549 addressed the relationship between the two subchapters.

GISRA, §1061 · Homeland Security Act, Title X · FISMA 2002, §301

FISMA 2014 replaced both earlier subchapters with §§3551–3558. It retained the agency-program and evaluation structure while expressly assigning DHS implementation responsibilities. The annual evaluation requirement therefore has a history extending before the current §3555 was enacted.

FISMA 2014, §2 · Compare the evaluation provisions

NIST’s standards work has a direct statutory basis

Congress assigned technical standards responsibilities separately from OMB’s management policy. The Computer Security Act added a computer-standards program to NIST’s governing statute. The 2002 laws revised that framework; today 15 USC §278g-3 assigns NIST standards and guidance work, while 40 USC §11331 governs Commerce’s issuance of federal information-system standards.

The two 2002 enactments also had different effective dates. The Code’s amendment notes preserve their interaction. In 2022, Congress amended the standards provisions to name Commerce in places that had named OMB. The current wording and earlier versions are available in the comparisons.

Computer Security Act, §3 · NIST provision’s amendment and effective-date notes · Compare standards authority

DHS received additional operational powers over time

FISMA 2014 expressly assigned DHS a role in administering agency security implementation, including binding operational directives. Congress added emergency-directive authority in 2015. Later legislation established CISA within DHS and added further operational duties. These changes are visible in §3553 and 6 USC §652.

Cybersecurity Act of 2015, §229 · CISA Act of 2018

FedRAMP moved from OMB policy into statute

OMB established FedRAMP through policy in 2011. Congress gave the program a statutory basis in 2022, adding §§3607–3616. The law incorporates selected definitions from A-130 and NIST, assigns responsibilities to GSA and agencies, and directs OMB to issue implementation guidance. OMB’s 2024 memorandum updates that policy framework.

2011 FedRAMP policy · FedRAMP Authorization Act, §5921 · OMB M-24-15
