Coverage, editions, and sources

Source text, explanations, and historical comparisons are stored separately. The links below identify the official files used for this edition.

What is included

All codified section text in Title 44 chapters 35 and 36 is included, with their source credits, editorial notes, and statutory notes. Connected provisions cover privacy, IGs, CISA, standards, CIOs, IT investment, and acquisition supply-chain security. The chapter readers show the full section text; each section page includes its notes.

The current-law text reproduces OLRC release 119-118, through September 30, 2026. It is a dated Code snapshot, not a representation that later enactments have been incorporated. Retrieval on October 10 does not change that release date. The official classification-table service was unavailable during collection; later-law verification remains a separate update step.

The A-130 guide uses the July 28, 2016 circular, identified by NIST’s A-130 index. Related documents retain the editions and status recorded in the source library.

How to interpret the history

The source credit identifies enactments and amendments to a Code section. It does not always identify the first appearance of a duty: Congress may restate or move an older provision. Official prior-provision and effective-date notes accompany the text.

Edition comparisons preserve the words of collected Code editions. They show changes between those editions, including editorial punctuation. They are not day-by-day reconstructions of every intervening amendment. Clause readings follow the same numbered locator; renumbered predecessors are shown separately in section comparisons. The 2000 and 2002 predecessors of the FISMA sections appear alongside the current sections.

Historical collection begins with available 1994 Code editions and selected later editions. Earlier enactments are linked from the historical report and source credits. Missing older readings are not treated as evidence that a duty did not exist.

Connections beyond the collection

Internal statutory cross-references link locally when the target is included. Other references link to the official Code. Related materials are selected implementation documents, not a catalogue of every policy, directive, standard, or agency-specific instruction.
