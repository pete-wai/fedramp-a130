# A-130 history and authority: local web report

## Summary

Build a simple, self-contained website for policy and technical leaders tracing federal computer-security policy from A-130’s predecessors through October 10, 2026. Combine a full research narrative with an interactive chronology, searchable document inventory, and downloaded original sources.

Deliver in `/Users/pwx/codex/fedramp-misc/a130-history/`. No hosting, accounts, or backend.

## Research and analysis

- Establish the complete A-130 revision history using original circulars, transmittals, and Federal Register records. Investigate the 1985, 1993, 1994, 1996, 2000, and 2016 milestones; distinguish proposals, amendments, consolidated texts, and final issuances.
- Write approximately 250–350 words per substantive update covering timing, purpose, changes, relevant laws, security implications, and contemporaneous NIST work.
- Trace relevant predecessors, including A-71 and its security transmittal, earlier OMB bulletins, and foundational statutory authorities. Extend earlier than the 1960s only where necessary to explain the authority chain.
- Analyze the principal statutory transitions, including the Brooks Act, Privacy Act, Paperwork Reduction Acts, Computer Security Act, Clinger-Cohen Act, GISRA, FISMA 2002 and 2014, and FedRAMP legislation. Include executive orders and directives where they establish a separate policy driver.
- Deeply examine NIST FIPS and SP publications that established or materially changed security governance, risk management, controls, authorization, cryptography, identity, contractor security, and cloud policy. Include relevant historical versions and withdrawals.
- Seek all identifiable OMB M-memos referencing or extending A-130. Provide dates, relevance, affected requirements, and documented supersession or rescission. Include significant unnumbered memoranda separately.
- Explain each relationship as explicit statutory mandate, explicit OMB direction, implementation support, later incorporation, or chronological association. Do not infer causation from publication order.
- Distinguish OMB policy and oversight, NIST technical responsibilities, Commerce promulgation of standards, and national-security exceptions. Treat independent NIST authority separately from an actual limitation on OMB authority.
- Assess “how much flows through A-130” qualitatively by policy domain, supported by evidence; avoid unsupported percentages.

## Website and source collection

- Use buildless HTML, CSS, and JavaScript that works directly from a local folder without network dependencies.
- Use a restrained report layout: readable typography, persistent section navigation, responsive tables, and print styling.
- Organize the report into executive synthesis, chronology, A-130 update summaries, authority analysis, NIST relationships, memorandum inventory, and sources/methodology.
- Provide a chronological view with aligned lanes for laws, OMB actions, and NIST publications. Selecting an event reveals its summary, documented relationships, citations, and source links.
- Provide searchable, sortable reference tables with filters for document type, era, authority relationship, and cloud/commercial-services/FedRAMP flags.
- Distinguish exact term matches from related terminology such as commercial providers, external services, and contractor-operated systems. Show matching passages with page or section references.
- Download every located original A-130 version and substantive amendment, plus available primary documents supporting the analysis. Preserve original PDFs or HTML; label scans, extracted text, and reconstructed viewing copies accurately.
- Include both local download links and official source URLs. Maintain a downloadable source manifest recording document identity, dates, provenance, retrieval date, local filename, and availability limitations.
- Keep the complete narrative readable without interacting with filters. Use stable section anchors for citations and navigation.

## Evidence and validation

- Prefer OMB and presidential archives, Federal Register/GovInfo, enacted statutes, and NIST publication archives. Use secondary accounts for discovery or explicitly labeled historical context.
- Cite substantive authority claims to exact sections or pages. Distinguish issuance, publication, effective, and compliance dates where they differ.
- Document searched collections and unresolved gaps; describe inventory completeness as bounded by those searches.
- Verify downloaded files, local links, citations, event ordering, and whether each claimed directive actually supports the stated relationship.
- Manually verify flagged passages, using OCR where needed; identify documents whose text could not be searched reliably.
- Test local opening, navigation, search, combined filters, sorting, downloads, keyboard access, narrow screens, and printing.
- Deliver the completed site with a short opening guide and clearly identified research limitations.
